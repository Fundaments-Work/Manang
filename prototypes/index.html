Quantum Thermodynamic Irreversibility and Exact Entropy Production Bounds

A. Sharma1, M. Chen2, K. R. Manandhar1
1Department of Applied Physics, Fundaments Lab · 2Quantum Institute

Abstract — We establish rigorous finite-time lower bounds on irreversible entropy generation in non-equilibrium open quantum systems undergoing non-Markovian driving. By mapping density matrix trajectories under Lie-algebraic generators, we prove that informational entropy dissipation converges strictly to Landauer limits in the macroscopic asymptotic limit.

1. Introduction

The quantification of entropy production far from thermodynamic equilibrium remains central to quantum information theory. Landauer’s principle established that erasing one bit of information dissipates at least $k_B T \ln 2$ heat into the thermal bath [1].

Recent advances in single-spin measurement allow direct observation of quantum trajectory fluctuations, motivating exact non-perturbative formulations.

2. Quantum Dynamics

Consider a closed system with Hamiltonian $H(t)$ coupled weakly to a Gibbs state reservoir at inverse temperature $\beta = (k_B T)^{-1}$. The density operator evolves according to:

$\rho(t) = \mathcal{U}(t) \rho(0) \mathcal{U}^\dagger(t)$ (14)

where $\mathcal{U}(t) = \mathcal{T} \exp(-i \int_0^t H(\tau) d\tau)$.
